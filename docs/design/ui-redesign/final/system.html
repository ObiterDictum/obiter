The Obiter system

One calm language for legal work: warm paper by default, layered charcoal by choice, a single slate-blue accent, and status that never relies on colour alone. Built for long days and dense documents, not for a demo.

Light (default) and dark pass AA · Inter and IBM Plex Mono, self-hosted

Hairlines and washes

Borders are not decoration. Buttons, cards, rows and pills do not carry one. A hairline separates a table head or a list where space alone cannot. Status tints are used for the icon tile and the one flag, never for a whole row.

Type

Exactly six levels, three weights. The interface is Inter; IBM Plex Mono is reserved for client references and code-like strings. Nothing is smaller than 12px, and only the page title and section title carry tracking.

Reading

The Supreme Court restated the approach to contractual interpretation in Wood v Capita Insurance Services Ltd [2017] UKSC 24. Construction is a unitary exercise: the court reads the instrument as a whole and gives weight to both language and commercial context.

The question is not what the parties intended, but what the words they used would convey to a reasonable person with the relevant background knowledge.

“The court must consider the quality of the drafting, the commercial consequences of one reading over another, and the purpose of the clause.”

68 characters per line. Reading surfaces never stretch to the full width of a wide monitor.

One left edge, one right edge, one rhythm. Every page is the same frame: a 48px gutter at 1440 and 32px at 1024, a content column that stops at 1120px, and an optional 320px rail whose top aligns with the first section title.

How to align a page

  1. Frame. .ob-content > .ob-wrap. The wrapper is the only left edge: breadcrumb, page title, section titles and the first content of each section all start on it.
  2. Head. .ob-page-head holds the page title and the actions. It ends 40px above the first section title, and the actions are vertically centred on the title.
  3. Sections. .ob-section > .ob-section-head (16px below) > content. Between sections, 40px above the next title.
  4. Rail. .ob-cols with a 320px second column. Its first card starts on the first section title's line, not below it.
  5. Inside a card. 24px padding everywhere; list rows and table rows are at least 52px tall with 16px vertical padding.

Supply Agreement

This Agreement is dated 12 January 2025 between Northgate Foods Limited (“the Buyer”) and Brightwater Packaging Limited (“the Supplier”).

4. Volumes

4.1 The Supplier shall supply the Products in accordance with each Order.

4.2 The Buyer shall order, and the Supplier shall supply, not less than 60,000 45,000 units of the Products in each calendar month during the Term.1

12. Liability

12.2 Subject to clause 12.1, each party's total liability in any Contract Year shall not exceed the Charges paid in that Contract Year paid in the preceding 12 months.2

Change bar. A 2px amber rule in the margin marks a paragraph with a revision. One per paragraph, never around the text.

Insertion and deletion. Green underline for added text, red strikethrough for removed text. Colour is paired with a line so it survives monochrome.

Markers. A 16px numbered circle ties a change or comment to the side panel. The author colour is a tint, not a fill.

Comment anchor. Only the number sits beside the text; the comment itself lives in the panel, not in a margin balloon.

Rules that are not tokens

  • One primary button per view. The primary is the next real decision, not “New matter”.
  • One loud flag per screen. Every other status is a dot and a word.
  • Never letter-space a filename or citation. Tracking belongs to the page title and section title only.
  • No uppercase labels. The type scale carries hierarchy.
  • One date format. 26 Oct 2026, and 26 Oct 2026, 14:32 when the time matters.
  • Never show a system ID. A matter reference is human and goes in mono; a version is “v2”.
  • Focus is always visible. 2px ring, 2px offset, in the brand hue.

What “immutable versions” means
A version is a locked copy. Once saved it cannot be changed, by anyone. Editing always creates a new version, so a copy you have shared stays exactly as it was.
